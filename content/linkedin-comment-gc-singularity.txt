MAIN (about 95 words):

The singularity is the wrong yardstick for operations, I think. Nobody on a corporate-actions desk needs the model to be smarter than a person. They need its number to be right, and to know who checked it. In my own evaluation work this summer, eight frontier models got the go/no-go decision right on the back-office cases I gave them and lost their points on the arithmetic underneath, with a fluent explanation attached to the wrong figure. That is not a singularity. It is a very capable colleague whose work still has to be tied out. The human role that survives is the one that reads the work.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
SHORT (about 60 words):

Whether the model is smarter than the analyst is the wrong question for operations. Whether its number is right, and who checked it, is the one that gets asked when something is paid. In my own evaluation runs this summer, the strong models got the decision right and slipped on the arithmetic underneath, fluently. The role that survives is the one that reads the work.
