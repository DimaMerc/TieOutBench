In April, US banking regulators rewrote model risk for the first time in fifteen years.

SR 11-7 - the rulebook that made every consequential model earn an inventory entry, an independent validation, and a paper trail - is gone. Its replacement, SR 26-2, is mostly sensible modernization. Except for one footnote: generative AI and agentic AI are explicitly out of scope. "Novel and rapidly evolving." Firms should govern them with their own risk practices.

So the rewrite arrived the same year AI agents started reconciling ledgers, drafting research, and watching NAVs - and declined to cover exactly those models.

Some firms are treating that as a pass: GenAI is "productivity software," no validation file needed. A few were reportedly validating anyway, even before the rewrite. I think the honest answer sits elsewhere: you can't validate a foundation model, but you can validate the work it does. Gold sets. Auto-fail gates. Challenger runs. Receipts.

I wrote up what that looks like in practice, including three findings from the gold cases and the live frontier-model runs that a decision-level spot check would have missed entirely.

Article in the first comment. If this debate is live at your firm, I'd love to hear how you're approaching it.
