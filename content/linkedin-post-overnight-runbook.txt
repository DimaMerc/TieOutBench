ALTERNATIVE FIRST PARAGRAPH (leads with the number instead of the date)
A Germany ETF traded 2,303 shares in sixty nights on the US overnight market. On December 6 the exchanges
are due to open that market 23 hours a day, so I measured what it looks like today: ten funds, sixty
nights, every trade against a value built from futures prices. Five results, each for a different team.

Expected questions and honest answers
- "Isn't 2 to 3 bps just the spread?" No: it is the typical distance of actual trades from the
  futures-implied value. The tape has trades only, so the report says nothing about spreads.
- "So the thin funds are mispriced at night?" The data does not show that. Their trades were far from a
  futures-only reference, and for emerging markets more than 40% of that distance was the reference
  missing the Asian markets. What the data does show is that they rarely trade.
- "Are you saying quoting obligations would fix it?" No. Frankfurt has them, an open primary market, an
  exact futures hedge and a main session, all at once; the data cannot separate these. It is the one of
  the four the US exchanges could choose to add.
- "Is 20% against 6 basis points a fair comparison?" They do different jobs: the band stops extreme prices,
  it does not estimate value. The point is that nothing published does the second job at night.
- "Which feed missed the ex-date?" The public chart feed named in the report's references. The issuer's
  own file had it; the report uses the issuer's dates.
- "Why didn't the news models help?" Because the futures and the fund's own trades already carried the
  information; four models, two cloud and two local, agreed on that.
- "What about after December 6?" Unknown; the same code captures the exchange sessions and compares the
  two periods, with Frankfurt as the control. Results in March.
