I test whether AI can be trusted with finance work: real documents, one task at a time, every answer checked against the source, every run published.

My second technical report is on SSRN. It measures the oldest control in operations, a maker and a checker, when both are AI.

- Six corporate-action cases on real events, eight models, one grader, four ways of working.
- With tools, 35 of 48 runs passed every check, up from 32 with the documents in a single prompt.
- With a second AI of the same model as checker, 27. With a stronger checker, 25.
- The reviewers rejected 20 of the 46 correct ledgers, and the makers revised their work every time. A stronger reviewer over-ruled ten correct refusals into invented numbers.
- The reviewer had the documents but not the rules. That is the finding, and it is the next protocol.

Fourteen pages, every verdict and every finding published. Link in the first comment.

Who gives your checker the rules the maker works to?
