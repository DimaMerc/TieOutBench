PostTrade 360 is on in Stockholm this week and Sibos follows at the end of the month. I am following from Boston, which mostly means reading the agendas - and the AI session titles read very differently in a row than one at a time.

Some titles sell autonomy. "Kill the exception queue: how autonomous AI is taking over post-trade operations." "Agentic operations for post trade: building the operating model that delivers board-ready ROI."

Others ask about control. "AI in post-trade operations: readiness, governance and control ahead of T+1." "Artificial Intelligence Operating Models - Cost Reduction Vs. Risk." "AI in Financial Services - Productivity Engine or Emerging Operational Risk?" And from the network itself, at Sibos: "Agentic AI: identity, control and compliance."

Put the two lists next to each other and one thing stands out. Nobody is asking whether to use AI in post-trade any more - that debate is over. What both sides are asking, in different words, is who validates the agent, and with what evidence.

If you are in Stockholm this week, what is the answer in the room?

B. Thu Sep 3 - "Show your work" (the human post; back-to-school day)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

My son starts school today. The homework rule in our house is the one I grew up with, and the one no kid has ever liked: show your work. You have the answer. Why does anyone need the journey?

It turns out I now say the same thing to AI. On one of the finance tasks I test, a model worked through a discount rate line by line, arrived at 12.62% at the end of its own working, and then wrote 12.31% in the answer box. Any parent who has checked homework knows this one: the work is right, the number copied into the box is not. At school it costs a point. In finance, the number in the box is the one that gets used.

A teacher catches that in a second, because reading the work is most of the job. Most checks on AI read only the answer.

Good luck to everyone doing first days this week, the kids and the parents pretending to be calm. And to everyone who still reads the work.
